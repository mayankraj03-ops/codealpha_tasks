      MODULE 01
      
What Is Phishing?

      
Phishing is a cyber attack where criminals pretend to be a trusted person, company, bank, website, or organization to trick you into revealing sensitive information or performing an unsafe action.

    


    

      

        
Why attackers use phishing

        
Phishing is cheap, fast, and doesn't require breaking through firewalls. It's easier to trick a person than to hack a system, so attackers target human trust instead of technical weaknesses.

      

      

        
What they try to steal

        
Passwords, one-time passcodes (OTPs), bank details, credit card numbers, company credentials, personal identity information, or access to your accounts and devices.

      

      

        
How attacks normally work

        
An attacker sends a message that looks legitimate, creates a reason for you to act quickly, and directs you to a fake website or attachment designed to capture your information.

      

      

        
Why it's dangerous

        
A single click can lead to stolen money, identity theft, compromised company networks, ransomware infections, or accounts being taken over entirely.

      MODULE 04
      
Fake Website Detection

      
Attackers build websites that look identical to the real thing. Learning to read a URL is one of the most powerful defenses you have.

    


    

      

        LEGITIMATE (EXAMPLE)
        https://secure.example.test/login
      

      

        FICTIONAL PHISHING EXAMPLE
        https://example-security-login.test
      

    


    

      ⚠ Important: HTTPS encrypts the connection between you and a website — it does not prove the website is legitimate. A phishing website can use HTTPS too. The padlock icon means "this connection is private," not "this site is trustworthy."

Typosquatting

        
Attackers register domains that are one letter off from the real one, like using the number "1" instead of the letter "l" — "examp1e.test" instead of "example.test".

      

      

        
Lookalike domains

        
Extra words are added to make a domain look official, such as "example-security-login.test" instead of the real "example.test".

      

      

        
URL shortening

        
Shortened links hide the real destination. Always be cautious of shortened links in unexpected messages, especially ones asking for personal information.

      

      

        
Redirects

        
A link may open a legitimate-looking page first, then silently redirect you to a fake login page designed to steal your credentials.
